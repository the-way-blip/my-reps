# 2026 general election (Nov 3) state data-source survey

Research date: 2026-10-28. Findings only, nothing was built.
Benchmarks: **Wisconsin** (one statewide XLSX of every contest) and **Indiana** (ENR pre-loaded with every contest at 0 votes, public JSON) = difficulty 1. **Iowa** (state lists only federal/state/legislative, so county races needed 99 auditor sites) = difficulty 5.

How each source was checked:
- **VERIFIED**: I fetched the URL with curl on 2026-10-28, got HTTP 200, and inspected the data.
- **PAGE-ONLY**: the page responds, but I did not inspect a 2026 dataset.
- **UNVERIFIED**: the claim rests on background knowledge or search snippets. Confirm before building.

## Notes per state

### Difficulty 1 (as easy as WI/IN)

**North Carolina.** This is the best find, a direct match for the Wisconsin model. `Candidate_Listing_2026.csv` holds both the 03/03 primary and the 11/03 general. Filter `election_dt == "11/03/2026"`. Columns: county_name, contest_name, name_on_ballot, party_candidate, vote_for, term, is_partisan, is_unexpired, plus contact info. Ballot order is not a column (NC orders by party, from a random drawing). The S3 folder also has PDFs: the statewide referendum text, local referendums, and the general candidate list by contest. For address-to-ballot mapping, NCSBE also publishes precinct/district shapefiles and a sample-ballot lookup (not checked here).

**Minnesota.** The election-night media files are already published for 20261103. They work like Indiana: every contest is pre-loaded, and the per-precinct file (`allracesbyprecinct.txt`, 27 MB) gives the precinct-to-contest mapping directly. Field layout: `state;county;precinct;office_id;office_name;district;cand_order;cand_name;suffix;incumbent;party;precincts_reporting;precincts_total;votes;pct;total_votes`. The statewide files (US Senate, governor, etc.) currently contain test vote totals. Local files are at 0. Every human-facing SOS host returns a Radware CAPTCHA to scripts, but `electionresultsfiles.sos.mn.gov` does not. I could not confirm whether there is a 2026 constitutional amendment.

**Louisiana.** The SOS results JSON is pre-loaded for 11/03/2026: 933 races and 10 constitutional amendments, plus parish and municipal propositions. Note that LA's Nov 3 ballot is the open primary for many local offices, with a December runoff, so some winners won't be final. Congress uses the new closed party primary system. `ParishesInElection.htm` gives precinct counts. Votes by precinct are under `VotesRaceByPrecinct/Votes_<raceId>.htm`.

**Virginia.** One XLSX lists every office, including the 2026 local races (towns, cities, school boards). It has office and party ballot order, locality, district and seats. The Enhanced Voting ENR is already created for the general. Constitutional amendments have their own page. 2026 is a federal-plus-local year, so the list is effectively the whole ballot.

**Maryland.** Two CSVs, county and statewide, together cover everything except local ballot questions (charter amendments, bonds), which appear only in the per-county sample-ballot PDFs. Municipal elections are separate and mostly odd-year.

**New Mexico and South Dakota.** Both use the same Civix-style ENR as NE and ND. They are pre-loaded for Nov 3, 2026, with county races and per-contest or per-county exports, but need HTML/ASP.NET export scraping instead of a clean JSON feed. South Dakota currently has test data loaded.

### Difficulty 2

**Kentucky.** All county, city and school-board filings are in one SOS database (county filings page), but it is HTML with ASP.NET postbacks (scrapable). The SOS warns that clerk filings arrive with lag. Constitutional Amendment 1 is listed.

**North Dakota.** `CandidateList.aspx?eid=348` is the 2026 General Contest/Candidate List, with county offices and soil conservation districts (HTML). The ENR has not switched to 2026 yet.

**Utah.** Enhanced Voting is pre-loaded (`General11032026`), and the state XLSX is downloadable at 0 votes. County contests need the 29 county-locality pulls on the same API. There is also an official address-based candidates-and-issues lookup.

**South Carolina.** VREMS has JSON endpoints for the election list (General 2026 = id 22596) and server-rendered candidate and referendum searches. Municipal Nov 3 elections are separate IDs.

**Nebraska.** The ENR is pre-loaded with Amendment 1 and Initiatives 440–442. I did not confirm that county officer races appear (none on the sampled county page). Check a county's "Local Results" tab.

**Pennsylvania and Connecticut.** Even-year ballots are federal, state and legislative only, so a state candidate list is ~complete. Both state sites gate curl, so the source URL still needs a manual find.

### Difficulty 3

**Georgia.** The ENR API works (Enhanced Voting) but the general is not loaded yet; it will likely appear on or near election night. The candidate qualifying site is behind Cloudflare. November local content is limited (partisan county races).

**Tennessee.** Same shape as PA: county races were in August. Federal, state and legislative lists plus 3 amendments, but November municipal races (Nashville and others) are held by counties. SOS site returns 403.

**KS, AR, NV, ID, MT, OK, AZ, WY, WV, NH.** Each has either a bot block or no confirmed statewide local list. See the table.

### Difficulty 4–5 (Iowa-like)

**CO, FL, OH, MO, IL, AL, TX.** The statewide list is limited to federal, state, legislative and judicial offices, while many county offices are on the 2026 November ballot and are held per county: CO 64 counties, FL 67, OH 88, MO 115, IL 102, AL 67, TX 254. Colorado and Florida at least have a clean statewide file for the upper ballot.
